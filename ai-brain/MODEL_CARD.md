# Model card: the AI Brain's shipped models

Generated by `training/train_shipped.py` (prompt 5.2); do not edit by hand. The same facts are in
`models/real/model_card.json`, which the service serves and the app shows.

## Trained on

- **LifeSnaps**: 69 participants, 3551 nights, Fitbit Sense. Yfantidou et al., LifeSnaps, a 4-month multi-modal dataset capturing unobtrusive snapshots of our lives in the wild. Scientific Data 9:663, 2022. https://zenodo.org/records/7229547, CC BY 4.0.
- PMData (CC BY-NC 4.0, non-commercial) is not in the shipped models, so the app carries no non-commercial term. The paper's replication on PMData is unchanged.
- Pipeline: training/train_shipped.py through datasets/ and training/train_real.py, as evaluation/run_study.py. Seed 42, scikit-learn 1.8.0, trained 2026-11-14T19:39:12+00:00.

Until prompt 5.2 the service shipped forests trained on a synthetic panel (6,878 simulated nights from 340
simulated people). Their metrics (wake AUC 0.785 on simulated people) said nothing about real people and are
no longer shown anywhere.

## Inputs

Used: `chronotype_code`, `bedtime_hour`, `exercise_minutes`, `resting_hr`, `steps`, `sleep_consistency`, `sleep_debt_hours`.

An input the app doesn't send takes the training nights' median, so it neither helps nor hurts. Medians: `chronotype_code` 1.0, `bedtime_hour` 24.3583, `exercise_minutes` 30.0, `resting_hr` 66.3123, `steps` 7871.0, `sleep_consistency` 63.8599, `sleep_debt_hours` 4.0.

Not used, though the app has them:

- `age`: LifeSnaps gives age only as a band (under or over 30), not a number.
- `screen_minutes_before_bed`: no public wearable dataset records it.
- `caffeine_mg`: no public wearable dataset records it.
- `stress_level`: LifeSnaps has it for only 53% of nights; the study leaves it out rather than lose half the participants (docs/sleep-duration-results.md).
- `ambient_noise_db`: no public wearable dataset records it.
- `room_temp_c`: no public wearable dataset records it.
- `snooze_count`: no public wearable dataset records it.
- `alarm_response_ms`: no public wearable dataset records it.

## How well they do, on people they never saw

Grouped by participant: 8 splits, 20% of people held out each time, never seen in training. Means with a 95% interval over the splits.

**Sleep duration** (3376 nights, 63 people):

- MAE 1.016 (95% interval 0.953 to 1.079; splits 0.921 to 1.170) hours; predicting everyone's average gives 1.197.
- R² 0.186 (95% interval 0.075 to 0.297; splits -0.077 to 0.395).

**Wake-time regularity** (3132 nights, 62 people). A night counts as regular unless the wake was more than 30 minutes later than the person's own habit (the median of their last 28 nights of the same kind, weekday or weekend; datasets/wake_proxy.py). It says nothing about how the morning went.

- AUC 0.660 (95% interval 0.610 to 0.710; splits 0.518 to 0.733); 0.5 is chance.
- Balanced accuracy 0.615 (95% interval 0.581 to 0.648; splits 0.541 to 0.678); 0.5 is chance.
- Accuracy 0.716 (95% interval 0.676 to 0.756; splits 0.623 to 0.787), against 0.748 for always guessing the commoner outcome (75% of held-out nights were regular).

These are the paper's numbers: the same cohort, features, forest and splits.

## Thresholds the app words its advice by

Off-schedule warning above 0.488; "usually close to your usual time" from 0.704 (75th percentiles of held-out predictions over the evaluation splits).

## No longer predicted

- `wakeup_consistency`: No real-data target exists for it, so the synthetic model is gone; the app shows your measured sleep consistency instead.

## Limits

- Weak in absolute terms: modestly better than chance at regularity, and duration explains under a fifth of the variance.
- 69 people with a Fitbit Sense (35 under 30, 31 aged 30 or over, 3 not given), May 2021 to January 2022: it may not describe you.
- Nothing it predicts was ever about alarms: no public dataset records an alarm, a snooze or a wake check.
- Screen time, caffeine, room noise and temperature can't move these predictions: no dataset recorded them.
