Getting SomniAI to a publishable paper. Not a product launch.

**Target:** a methods paper validated on public sleep datasets. Submission in

## The one thing that decides whether this gets published

Reviewers do not read your code. They read your evaluation.

The idea is sound and the implementation is already better built than most work
that gets accepted. What would sink it today is a single sentence any reviewer
would write: *"the system is evaluated only on synthetic data generated by the
authors."*

So the work ahead is **evaluation, not features**. Every prompt below either
produces evidence a reviewer will weigh, or protects the correctness of a number
you are going to report. Nothing else earns its place.

- [x] **7. Design the wake-success proxy** — *done, with a negative result that
  changes the paper's framing. Full write-up in
  [`docs/wake-proxy-validation.md`](docs/wake-proxy-validation.md).*

  `ai-brain/datasets/wake_proxy.py`, 27 unit tests + 7 real-data checks.

  night-by-night agreement across settings is 0.86–0.94. Labels 89% of LifeSnaps
  nights (rate 0.746) and 95% of PMData (0.666).

  **Circularity: passes.** Correlation with `sleep_consistency` is +0.089 /
  +0.053 — the label is not a restatement of a model input.

  readiness or tiredness (all |d| < 0.1, two of three the wrong way). It does
  track objective timing (corr with sleep duration +0.18 / +0.37).

  **Consequence:** waking later than habit is not a bad outcome — most late
  wakes are lie-ins. The target must be renamed from *wake success* to
  **wake-time regularity**, predicting P(waking within N minutes of habitual
  wake time). The inverse-planning method is unchanged; only the claim changes.
  Steps 9 onward and the manuscript should use the new framing.

## Phase 2 — Retrain and report honestly

- [x] **8. Retrain the sleep-duration model** — *done. The gap is far larger
  than "worse". Write-up in
  [`docs/sleep-duration-results.md`](docs/sleep-duration-results.md).*

  `ai-brain/training/train_real.py`, 22 unit tests + 4 real-data checks.

  | | MAE (h) | R² | vs mean baseline |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Synthetic panel (same 7 features) | 0.403 | +0.702 | +45.6% |
  | LifeSnaps (mean of 8 splits) | 1.016 | +0.186 | +15.0% |
  | PMData | 1.072 | **−0.090** | +3% |

  **The synthetic panel overstates predictability roughly 4× in R².** On PMData
  the model is worse than predicting the mean. Across LifeSnaps splits R² runs
  −0.08 to +0.40, so no single-split figure is a result. An oracle using each
  test participant's own mean beats the model in 5 of 8 splits — most
  predictable variance is between people, not between nights within a person.

  Two guards, both tested: splits are by participant (asserted across 15 seeds),
  and `assert_no_leakage` refuses `time_in_bed_hours`, `sleep_efficiency` and
  `wake_time_hour` as predictors, since duration is nearly time-in-bed times
  efficiency.

  **Consequence:** the planner inverts a model explaining ~19% of variance.
  Report planner behaviour conditional on that, and treat frequent
  infeasibility verdicts as correct behaviour rather than a defect.

- [x] **9. Retrain the wake-success model** — *done. The off-ramp triggered.
  Write-up in [`docs/wake-regularity-results.md`](docs/wake-regularity-results.md).*

  | | AUC | Balanced acc. | Accuracy | Majority | Δ |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Synthetic | 0.761 | 0.700 | 0.699 | 0.515 | +18.4 pts |

## Framing decision (2026-09-10)

Steps 7–9 all point one way: **wrist-wearable features predict sleep outcomes
far more weakly than a synthetic panel implies.** The label tracks schedule
regularity rather than wellbeing; sleep duration falls from R² 0.70 to 0.19;
wake regularity from AUC 0.76 to 0.66.

**Chosen framing: infeasibility-first.** The contribution is a planner that
knows when it cannot promise. Weak models are the *motivation*, not a
limitation to apologise for — most systems emit a number regardless of whether
they can support it, and this one declines. The bedtime recommendation is a
secondary output; the refusal and its attribution are the claim.

This changes what steps 10–12 measure. Bedtime-accuracy baselines are no longer
the point; **calibration and refusal quality are.**

## Phase 3 — Build the evidence a reviewer wants

> Rewritten for the infeasibility-first framing. The old versions measured
> bedtime accuracy, which the step 8–9 results no longer support as a headline.

- [x] **10. Calibration and refusal quality** — *done. Write-up in
  [`docs/calibration-refusal-results.md`](docs/calibration-refusal-results.md).*

  `ai-brain/evaluation/planner_eval.py`, 22 tests.

  **Calibration holds: ECE 0.0148** over 3,180 held-out person-nights. The 0.93
  bin observes 0.929. Reported with Brier (0.1727 vs 0.1898 for a base-rate
  predictor) because always predicting the base rate scores ECE 0.0000 —
  calibrated and useless. Honest summary: **well calibrated, weakly informative.**

  **Refusal beats always-promising decisively.** Over-promise rate at target
  0.95: always-promise and cycle-calculator 1.000, inverse 0.040. At 0.90:
  0.867 against 0.068.

  **But inversion itself is not testable here.** `forward_only` scores better
  than `inverse` at every target, and that is a measurement artifact: forward
  predicts the night that actually happened, while inversion promises
  conditional on a bedtime change nobody made. The counterfactual is
  unobservable in retrospective data. Validated: calibration, and refusal versus
  always-promising. Not testable: the bedtime recommendation itself.

  *Also fixed here:* `datasets/cache.py`. Parsing LifeSnaps costs ~296 s per run
  because of the 9.7 GB mongodump; cached reads take 0.07 s. Every experiment
  was paying that, which made the planner look slow when it was not.

- [x] **11a. Validate the inversion in simulation** — *done. Write-up in
  [`docs/simulation-validation-results.md`](docs/simulation-validation-results.md).*

  `ai-brain/evaluation/simulation.py` + `scenarios.py`, 17 tests. Seven
  ground-truth models × six targets, graded against exhaustive search.

  | Property | Result |
  | --- | --- |
  | Bedtime sweep finds the latest feasible bedtime | **100%** (42/42) |
  | Promises only what is reachable (**soundness**) | **100%** — 0 violations |
  | Lever set exactly minimal when it succeeds | **100%** (13/13) |
  | Refuses only the genuinely unreachable (**completeness**) | 76% — 10 false refusals |

  **Sound but incomplete** — it never over-promises, but is conservative. The
  cause is the iteration budget, not the greedy strategy: a traced case needed
  4 rounds and `max_rounds=3` stopped it one round short of the target.

  Raising `max_rounds` 3 → 8 cuts false refusals from 10 to 3 and **never
  introduces a broken promise**. Recommended, left unchanged pending your
  decision since it alters production behaviour.

- [x] **11b. Matched-subset analysis and ablations** — *done. Write-up in
  [`docs/ablation-matched-results.md`](docs/ablation-matched-results.md).*

  `ai-brain/evaluation/ablation.py` + `matched.py`, 16 tests.

  **Ablations — every component has a distinct job:**

  | Ablation | Verdict correct | False refusals | Broken promises | Bedtime optimal |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | full planner | 0.929 | 3 | **0** | 1.00 |
  | no bedtime sweep | 0.881 | 5 | 0 | **0.69** |
  | no coordinate ascent | **0.762** | **10** | 0 | 1.00 |
  | no controllable/habit partition | 0.857 | 3 | **3** | 1.00 |
  | no attribution | 0.929 | 3 | 0 | 1.00 |

  The sweep buys optimality (minimality 0.95 → 0.56 without it), ascent buys
  completeness, and **the partition is the only component whose removal breaks
  soundness** — the mechanism the invention disclosure called distinctive turns
  out to be the one keeping the planner honest. Attribution changes no verdict,
  only whether the user is told why (2 habit causes named vs 0).

  **Matched subset — inconclusive, and that is the finding.** Direction positive
  at all four tolerances (d 0.17–0.40) but every CI crosses zero, with only 9–11
  participants contributing a paired difference. Underpowered by construction,
  and not randomised.

  Its real value is sizing the next study: detecting an effect this size needs
  **~50–75 participants**. `PRODUCT_PLAN.md` Phase F currently proposes 15–30,
  which would be underpowered — update it before running anything.

- [x] **12. Statistics** — *done. Write-up in
  [`docs/statistics-results.md`](docs/statistics-results.md).*

  `ai-brain/evaluation/statistics.py`, 17 tests. All uncertainty comes from
  resampling **participants**, never rows — a participant contributes ~50
  correlated nights.

  **The signal survives proper intervals.** R² [0.075, 0.297] excludes zero and
  AUC [0.610, 0.710] excludes chance, so "weak but real" is now supported rather
  than asserted from point estimates that swung −0.077 to +0.395 across splits.

  **Calibration is less precise than it looked.** ECE 0.0148 carries a
  bootstrap interval reaching **0.057** — still good, but the point estimate
  alone overstated it.

  **The two outcomes have different structure.** Unconditional ICC is 0.336 for
  sleep duration (a third is a stable person-level trait, formalising step 8's
  oracle result) but only 0.060 for wake regularity. Wake regularity is almost
  entirely night-to-night, which arguably makes it the better target for a
  system that intervenes on nights.

  Two predictors survive in both models by different estimators: later bedtime
  (−0.295 SD of sleep; OR 0.68 for regularity) and resting heart rate. **No
  habit feature reaches significance in either.**

  *Trap recorded:* the first fit reported ICC 0.000 with intervals spanning ±6.5
  million. `lbfgs` reported convergence while sitting on the boundary; `powell`
  and an independent ANOVA both find the variance is real. Models now run under
  `powell` with a `boundary_solution` flag and a test pinning the choice.

## Rules

- **One prompt at a time.** Each step's verification protects the next.
- **Evaluation beats features.** If a proposed piece of work doesn't change a
  number in the paper or protect one, it doesn't belong in this plan.
- **Report what you find.** Real-data results that are worse than synthetic are
  a finding. Tuning until the numbers look good is how papers get retracted.
- **Never report a number no test protects.**
